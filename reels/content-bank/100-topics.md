# Fire Claims Decoded: strategy and 100-topic script bank

## Part 1: How to make this niche work on Instagram

### Why it's open

Fire safety accounts exist (fire services, equipment sellers) and insurance
accounts exist (agents selling policies). Almost nobody explains **why fires
happen through the lens of the claims they cause**. That is your angle: an
engineer in property insurance who has seen what actually burns, and what
gets paid and what doesn't.

### The four content pillars

| pillar | what it is | why it travels |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cause decoded | One occupancy or one cause: how the fire starts, why it spreads, 3 fixes | Saves: people keep checklists |
| 2. Claims decoded | Why a claim gets cut, delayed or rejected | Shares: people forward money warnings |
| 3. Real incident, real lesson | A documented fire, what went wrong, what would have stopped it | Watch time: story keeps people |
| 4. Myth vs fact | A common belief, broken in 20 seconds | Comments: people argue |

Rotate them: never two of the same pillar in a row.

### The shape of every reel (30 to 40 seconds)

1. **Hook (0 to 3s):** a command, a number or a reveal. Motion in frame 1.
2. **Stake (3 to 7s):** why the viewer should care, in money or safety.
3. **Mechanism (7 to 20s):** the engineer's "why". This is your edge.
4. **Fix (20 to 32s):** 2 or 3 actions, each with an on-screen card.
5. **Ask (last 3s):** one question or one save. Echo a word from the hook.

### Rules that keep the account credible

- Never name a customer, insurer or claim you handled.
- Real incidents: only what police, fire services or official reports said.
  No death counts unless confirmed; no blame on victims.
- Insurance points: say "check your policy wording". Wordings differ by
  insurer and policy; never promise what a policy pays.
- Check every on-screen word in AI video. Garbled text kills trust.
- Run `/ig-reel <topic>` on any topic below before shooting: it scores the
  hook and times the script. The scripts below are drafts, not yet scored.

### Cadence

3 reels a week covers these 100 in about 8 months. Suggested week:
Mon cause decoded, Wed claims decoded or myth, Fri real incident.
Tie topics to the calendar: Diwali (crackers, diyas), summer (AC, transformers),
monsoon (water in panels), winter (heaters, angithis), wedding season (pandals).

### What to measure

Use `/ig-audit` monthly. The numbers that matter for this niche: **saves**
(checklist value), **shares/sends** (people warning family), and **comments**
on the closing question. Views alone will mislead you.

## Part 2: 100 scripts

Format: **Hook** / body / **Ask**. About 35 to 45 seconds each when spoken.
Topics marked ⚠ contain facts to verify on the day you shoot.

### A. Home fires

**1. Phone charging on the bed**
**Hook:** Stop charging your phone under your pillow.
A charging phone gets warm. Under a pillow, that heat has nowhere to go. A damaged cable or a cheap charger makes it worse, and bedding catches easily.
Charge on a hard surface. Use the original or certified charger. Replace frayed cables.
**Ask:** Where is your phone charging right now?

**2. Mosquito coil near the curtain**
**Hook:** That mosquito coil is a slow fire you leave burning all night.
A coil smoulders for hours. Put it near a curtain, bedsheet or paper and one gust of fan air is enough.
Use it on a metal stand, on the floor, away from anything that burns. Or switch to a plug-in or net.
**Ask:** Coil, liquid or net: what does your home use?

**3. Pooja diya and agarbatti**
**Hook:** The most common fire in Indian homes starts in the pooja room.
Open flame, cotton wicks, flowers, wooden mandir, synthetic curtains, all within a metre.
Put the diya on a metal plate. Keep cloth and garlands away. Never leave it burning when you go out.
**Ask:** Is your diya on wood or metal?

**4. LPG cylinder leak at home**
**Hook:** Smell gas? Don't touch a single switch.
A switch makes a tiny spark. In a room full of LPG, that is enough to ignite it.
Close the regulator. Open doors and windows. Leave. Call your gas agency from outside. Change the rubber hose before its expiry date.
**Ask:** When did you last check the date on your gas hose?

**5. Room heater in winter**
**Hook:** A room heater needs one metre of empty space. Most don't get it.
Heaters ignite blankets, curtains and clothes left to dry on them.
Keep one metre clear on all sides. Plug it directly into the wall, not an extension board. Switch it off before you sleep.
**Ask:** Does your heater stay on all night?

**6. Clothes iron left on**
**Hook:** One forgotten iron can burn a whole room.
An iron left face-down on cloth or an ironing board heats it past its ignition point.
Always stand it upright. Unplug it the moment you finish. Buy one with auto shut-off.
**Ask:** Has your family ever left the iron on?

**7. Smoking in bed**
**Hook:** A cigarette in bed is the fire you sleep through.
A dropped cigarette can smoulder in a mattress for a long time before flames appear, and smoke reaches you first.
Never smoke in bed or when drowsy. Use deep, heavy ashtrays. Wet the butts before binning.
**Ask:** Would you know if a mattress was smouldering?

**8. Kitchen chimney grease at home**
**Hook:** Your kitchen chimney filter might be the most flammable thing in your house.
Every tadka sends oil up. It sticks in the filter. One flare from the pan reaches it.
Wash filters every 2 to 4 weeks. Never leave hot oil unattended. Keep a lid nearby.
**Ask:** When did you last wash your chimney filter?

**9. Balcony storage**
**Hook:** Your balcony is not a godown.
Old cartons, newspapers, furniture and plastic on a balcony catch a falling diya, a cracker or a neighbour's cigarette.
Clear it. Store only what you need, and nothing that burns easily.
**Ask:** What's sitting on your balcony right now?

**10. Smoke detector at home**
**Hook:** Most home fire deaths happen while people sleep. A ₹500 device wakes you.
Smoke reaches you before flames do, and smoke doesn't wake a sleeping person reliably.
Fit a smoke alarm outside bedrooms. Test it every month. Change the battery every year.
**Ask:** Does your home have even one smoke alarm?

### B. Electrical fires

**11. Overloaded extension board** (already made: Episode 02)

**12. Loose connections**
**Hook:** The most dangerous electrical fault doesn't trip your MCB.
A loose connection makes heat, not high current. The MCB sees nothing. The terminal glows quietly.
Signs: a warm switch, flickering lights, a burning smell, brown marks on a socket. Get terminals tightened once a year.
**Ask:** Do you have a switch that feels warm?

**13. Old aluminium wiring**
**Hook:** If your building is 30 years old, its wiring might be older than your fire safety rules.
Old wiring was designed for a fan and a bulb, not ACs, geysers and induction cooktops.
Get a load assessment. Upgrade cable sizes. Replace old switchboards.
**Ask:** How old is the wiring in your home?

**14. MCB vs ELCB/RCCB**
**Hook:** Your MCB protects the wire. It doesn't protect you.
An MCB trips on overload or short circuit. An RCCB trips on earth leakage, which causes shocks and many fires.
Ask your electrician to add an RCCB on your main board.
**Ask:** Does your home have an RCCB? Check today.

**15. Rats and wiring**
**Hook:** Rats cause fires. Literally.
Rodents chew cable insulation. Exposed conductors touch, spark, and ignite dust or packing material.
Seal cable entries. Use conduits. Do pest control in panels, ceilings and godowns.
**Ask:** Have you seen chewed wires anywhere?

**16. AC fires in summer**
**Hook:** Summer fires spike for one reason: ACs running 18 hours a day.
Compressors, capacitors and loose terminals overheat under constant load. Dust in outdoor units makes it worse.
Service ACs before summer. Use correctly rated wire and an MCB per AC. Don't run them through extension boards.
**Ask:** When was your AC last serviced?

**17. Electrical panel used as storage**
**Hook:** Your electrical panel room is not a store room.
Cartons and files near panels catch the first spark. And nobody can reach the switch in an emergency.
Keep 1 metre clear in front of every panel. Lock it. Nothing stored inside.
**Ask:** What's stored next to your main panel?

**18. Thermal imaging**
**Hook:** You can see an electrical fire before it starts.
A thermal camera shows hot spots on panels and terminals while everything still works.
For shops, offices and factories: get a thermography scan every year. It costs far less than one fire.
**Ask:** Has your workplace ever done a thermal scan?

**19. Cheap chargers and adapters**
**Hook:** That ₹99 charger has no protection inside.
Uncertified chargers skip the parts that cut power when things overheat.
Buy BIS-certified chargers and adapters. Check for the ISI or BIS mark. Throw away anything that gets hot.
**Ask:** Do you know where your charger was made?

**20. E-bike and EV battery charging**
**Hook:** Never charge your e-scooter in the staircase.
Lithium battery fires burn very hot, release toxic smoke and are hard to put out. A staircase is your escape route.
Charge in open, ventilated areas. Use the original charger. Never charge a damaged or swollen battery.
**Ask:** Where does your building charge e-scooters?

### C. Shops, offices and commercial

**21. Restaurant kitchen** (already made: restaurant kitchen fires)

**22. Garment shop**
**Hook:** A clothes shop is fuel, stacked to the ceiling.
Fabric, plastic covers and cardboard everywhere, plus decorative lights and old wiring.
Keep lights away from stock. Don't block the exit with racks. Keep an extinguisher by the counter.
**Ask:** How many exits does your local market shop have?

**23. Coaching centres** ⚠
**Hook:** Your child's coaching centre might have one exit.
Many run on upper floors with flex boards, AC units and a single narrow staircase.
Parents: check the exits, the extinguishers, and whether windows open. Ask for the fire NOC.
**Ask:** Have you ever checked your child's coaching centre?

**24. Hospitals and oxygen**
**Hook:** In a hospital, oxygen turns a small spark into a big fire.
Oxygen-enriched air makes things burn faster and hotter. ICUs have dozens of electrical devices running at once.
Maintain wiring and AC loads. Train staff on evacuation of patients who can't walk. Check oxygen line leaks.
**Ask:** Do hospitals near you run fire drills?

**25. Hotels and banquet halls**
**Hook:** Before you sleep in a hotel, count the doors to the exit.
In smoke you can't see. Counting doors gets you out.
Read the evacuation plan behind the door. Check the corridor exits. Never use the lift in a fire.
**Ask:** Do you check the exit when you check in?

**26. Shopping malls and food courts**
**Hook:** A mall has hundreds of fire risks under one roof.
Food court kitchens, decorative lights, storage behind shops and huge crowds.
Know your nearest exit. Notice if fire doors are kept locked or blocked. Report it.
**Ask:** Have you seen a blocked fire exit in a mall?

**27. Office server rooms**
**Hook:** Water can't fight a server room fire.
Electrical equipment, cables and batteries need special suppression, and the data loss can cost more than the fire.
Use clean-agent suppression, keep UPS batteries ventilated, and back up offsite.
**Ask:** Where is your company's backup stored?

**28. Rooftop restaurants** ⚠
**Hook:** Rooftop restaurants trap you above the fire.
Temporary structures, bamboo, fabric, open flames and hookah coals on a roof with one staircase.
Check the exits before you sit. Avoid places with flammable decor and blocked stairs.
**Ask:** Would you know how to get off that roof?

**29. Petrol pumps**
**Hook:** At a petrol pump, your phone isn't the danger. Static is.
Fuel vapour ignites easily. Static from getting in and out of a car can spark it.
Switch off the engine. Don't smoke. Touch metal before touching the nozzle.
**Ask:** Did you know static was the real risk?

**30. Small factories in residential areas**
**Hook:** That small unit in your lane may be storing chemicals next to homes.
Illegal units mix flammable stock, poor wiring and residents, with no fire exits.
Report unlicensed units to the local authority. Know what's operating near you.
**Ask:** Is there a factory unit in your residential lane?

### D. Industrial and warehouse

**31. Warehouse housekeeping** (already made: poor housekeeping and combustible storage)

**32. Combustible dust**
**Hook:** Flour can explode.
Fine dust of flour, sugar, wood, coal or cotton hanging in air ignites like a gas, and the first blast throws up more dust for a second.
Clean dust from beams and ledges. Use dust collection. Control sparks and hot surfaces.
**Ask:** Did you know dust could explode?

**33. Hot work: welding and cutting**
**Hook:** One welding spark can travel 10 metres.
Sparks drop through gaps and land on stock, then smoulder for hours after the welder leaves.
Use a hot work permit. Clear combustibles. Post a fire watch for at least an hour after.
**Ask:** Does your workplace use hot work permits?

**34. Cotton and textile mills**
**Hook:** A cotton bale can burn inside for days.
Cotton fires smoulder deep in bales and reignite after they look out.
Keep bale storage separated, control lint and dust, and pull apart and check burnt bales completely.
**Ask:** Have you seen a cotton fire reignite?

**35. Cold storage insulation**
**Hook:** A cold storage can burn fast.
Insulated panels with plastic foam cores, ammonia refrigeration and long wiring runs in the cold.
Use fire-rated panel cores, maintain refrigeration electricals, and watch for ammonia leaks.
**Ask:** Did you expect a cold room to burn?

**36. Plastic and packaging stock**
**Hook:** Plastic burns twice as dangerous as wood.
It burns hotter, melts and drips fire, and gives off thick toxic smoke.
Store plastics separately, keep sprinklers designed for plastics, and limit stack height.
**Ask:** What's the most flammable stock in your workplace?

**37. Spontaneous combustion**
**Hook:** Some things catch fire with no spark at all.
Oily rags, damp hay, coal and some oilseed cakes heat up on their own as they oxidise or ferment.
Keep oily rags in a closed metal bin. Monitor temperature of stored bulk material. Don't stack damp stock.
**Ask:** Did you know a rag could set itself on fire?

**38. Forklift and battery charging areas**
**Hook:** The forklift charging corner is a hidden risk in most warehouses.
Batteries give off hydrogen when charging. Hydrogen needs very little energy to ignite.
Charge in a ventilated, separated area. Keep stock away. No smoking.
**Ask:** Where are forklifts charged in your warehouse?

**39. Flammable liquids storage**
**Hook:** Thinner, petrol and solvents should never sit in a normal store room.
Their vapours spread along the floor and find a spark far away.
Use a flammable storage cabinet. Keep small quantities. Ventilate. Label everything.
**Ask:** Where are solvents stored at your workplace?

**40. Solar panels on factory roofs**
**Hook:** Solar panels can start roof fires.
Faulty connectors, damaged cables and DC arcing on combustible roofs.
Use certified installers and connectors. Get the system inspected yearly. Mark DC isolators clearly for firefighters.
**Ask:** Is your solar system ever inspected?

### E. Festivals and seasons in India

**41. Diwali cracker shops** (already made: cracker shop and godown fires)

**42. Diwali at home: diyas and lights**
**Hook:** Diwali lights and diyas on the same curtain? That's a fire setup.
Cheap light strings overheat; diyas sit next to cloth.
Use certified lights, keep diyas on metal or stone away from fabric, switch off lights at night.
**Ask:** How long do your Diwali lights stay on?

**43. Wedding pandals** ⚠
**Hook:** A wedding pandal can burn in minutes.
Fabric, bamboo, decorative lights, generators, and cooking in one temporary structure.
Ask the decorator for fire-retardant fabric, keep generators and kitchens separate, keep two clear exits.
**Ask:** Would you ask your decorator about fire safety?

**44. Holi and synthetic colours**
**Hook:** Some Holi colours are flammable powders.
Fine powders in the air near a flame can flash.
Keep colour play away from fires, bonfires and cooking. Buy safe colours.
**Ask:** Did you know colour powder could catch fire?

**45. Lohri and bonfires**
**Hook:** Bonfire night needs one bucket of water. Most don't have it.
Wind carries embers to roofs, vehicles and dry leaves.
Light it away from structures, keep water and sand ready, and put it out completely.
**Ask:** Who puts out the bonfire in your colony?

**46. Summer: transformer fires**
**Hook:** Summer heat pushes transformers past their limit.
Overload plus high temperature, plus garbage burning underneath.
Don't dump or burn waste near transformers. Report oil leaks and sparking to the DISCOM.
**Ask:** Is there garbage under the transformer in your area?

**47. Monsoon: water and electricity**
**Hook:** Monsoon causes electrical fires too.
Water entering panels and meter boxes causes short circuits and tracking.
Seal panels, raise meters above flood level, and switch off supply if water enters.
**Ask:** Is your meter box above flood level?

**48. Winter: angithi and charcoal**
**Hook:** An angithi in a closed room is a fire risk and a silent killer.
Charcoal makes carbon monoxide: no smell, no colour. And embers can ignite bedding.
Never sleep with it in a closed room. Ventilate. Keep it on a non-combustible base.
**Ask:** Does anyone you know sleep with an angithi on?

**49. Navratri and garba venues**
**Hook:** Thousands of people. One exit. Many lights.
Large crowds under temporary roofs with heavy electrical decoration.
Organisers: separate exits, certified electricians, extinguishers. Visitors: know your exit.
**Ask:** Did your garba venue have more than one exit?

**50. Ganpati pandals and processions**
**Hook:** Pandal lighting is temporary. The fire risk is permanent.
Temporary wiring, overloaded boards, open flames for aarti.
Use a licensed electrician, proper earthing, and keep aarti flames away from decorations.
**Ask:** Who wired your local pandal?

### F. Special hazards

**51. Vapour cloud explosion** (prompts already made)

**52. Gas cylinders in commercial kitchens**
**Hook:** Restaurant LPG cylinders belong outside the kitchen.
Cylinders next to burners mean a leak meets a flame instantly.
Keep cylinder banks outside in a ventilated enclosure, with a manifold and leak detector.
**Ask:** Where are the cylinders kept in your favourite restaurant?

**53. Paint shops**
**Hook:** A paint shop is a solvent store with a counter.
Thinners, spray cans and paint vapour plus electrical fittings.
No smoking, ventilate, keep stock away from lights and switches, keep a foam or CO2 extinguisher.
**Ask:** Have you seen anyone smoke in a paint shop?

**54. Chemical godowns**
**Hook:** Two chemicals that are safe alone can start a fire together.
Oxidisers stored next to fuels or acids can react and ignite.
Segregate by compatibility, keep safety data sheets, label, and train staff.
**Ask:** Does your store separate chemicals by type?

**55. Lithium battery warehouses**
**Hook:** A lithium battery fire can restart after it's "out".
Thermal runaway spreads cell to cell and can reignite.
Store at safe charge levels, separate stock, keep damaged batteries isolated, and plan with fire experts.
**Ask:** Did you know a battery fire could reignite?

**56. Grain silos and rice mills**
**Hook:** Rice husk and grain dust burn and can explode.
Dust plus hot bearings or electrical sparks in enclosed spaces.
Clean dust, maintain bearings and belts, and use dust-proof electrical fittings.
**Ask:** Have you worked near a rice mill or silo?

**57. Hookah lounges**
**Hook:** Every hookah has a hot coal on top.
Coals dropped on cushions, curtains and rugs.
Keep coals in metal containers, use fire-retardant furnishings, extinguish coals in water.
**Ask:** Would you notice a fallen coal in a dark lounge?

**58. Generator rooms**
**Hook:** Your DG set sits on fuel.
Diesel leaks, hot exhaust pipes and poor ventilation.
Contain leaks with a bund, keep the exhaust insulated and away from combustibles, service regularly.
**Ask:** Is your building's DG room ever cleaned?

**59. Car fires**
**Hook:** Most car fires don't start in an accident.
Fuel leaks, faulty aftermarket wiring and overheating engines.
Use authorised fitments, fix fuel smells immediately, keep a small extinguisher in the car.
**Ask:** Has anyone added extra wiring to your car?

**60. Tall buildings and cladding** ⚠
**Hook:** A building's outside wall can carry fire up 20 floors.
Combustible cladding and insulation let fire climb the face of the building.
Use non-combustible cladding, fire stops between floors, and check what your building uses.
**Ask:** Do you know what your building's facade is made of?

### G. Fire protection that fails

**61. Expired extinguishers**
**Hook:** Check the date on your fire extinguisher. Most people never have.
A depressurised or expired extinguisher won't work when you need it.
Check the gauge monthly, get it serviced yearly, refill after any use.
**Ask:** When was your extinguisher last refilled?

**62. Wrong extinguisher**
**Hook:** The wrong extinguisher can make a fire worse.
Water on oil or electrical fires spreads them or shocks you.
Know the classes: water for paper and wood, CO2 for electrical, foam or wet chemical for oil.
**Ask:** Which extinguisher do you have at home?

**63. PASS technique**
**Hook:** Most people use a fire extinguisher wrong the first time.
They aim at the flames, not the base, and run out in seconds.
Pull the pin, Aim at the base, Squeeze, Sweep side to side.
**Ask:** Have you ever actually used one?

**64. Sprinklers turned off**
**Hook:** A sprinkler system with its valve shut is decoration.
Valves get closed for maintenance and never reopened.
Lock valves open, check them monthly, and test the system as per standard.
**Ask:** Who checks the sprinkler valve in your building?

**65. Fire doors wedged open**
**Hook:** A fire door works only when it's closed.
Wedged open for convenience, it lets smoke into the staircase.
Never wedge fire doors. Fit hold-open devices that release on alarm.
**Ask:** Is your building's fire door propped open right now?

**66. Locked fire exits**
**Hook:** A locked fire exit is the most dangerous door in a building.
Many deadly fires had exits locked to stop theft or entry.
Use panic bars that open from inside. Never chain or padlock exit doors.
**Ask:** Have you ever seen a locked fire exit?

**67. Hydrant without water**
**Hook:** Many building fire hydrants are dry.
Pumps not maintained, tanks empty, hoses missing.
Test pumps weekly, keep the tank full, run a hose test, keep hose reels complete.
**Ask:** Has your society ever tested its hydrant?

**68. Fire alarm ignored**
**Hook:** False alarms teach people to ignore real ones.
Badly maintained detectors keep going off, so people stop reacting.
Maintain detectors, investigate every alarm, and evacuate every time.
**Ask:** What do people do when your office alarm rings?

**69. Fire NOC**
**Hook:** A fire NOC is not a one-time certificate.
It must be renewed, and the building must keep matching what was approved.
Check your building's NOC date and whether changes since then were approved.
**Ask:** Does your building have a valid fire NOC?

**70. Fire drills**
**Hook:** In a real fire, people do what they've practised.
Without a drill, people use the lift, go back for belongings, or freeze.
Run at least one drill a year at home and at work. Agree on an assembly point.
**Ask:** When was your last fire drill?

### H. Insurance claims decoded

**71. Underinsurance**
**Hook:** You can be insured and still get only half your loss paid.
If the sum insured is lower than the real value, many policies cut the claim in proportion. Check your policy wording.
Update sum insured every year, especially after buying stock or machinery.
**Ask:** When did you last update your sum insured?

**72. Stock declaration**
**Hook:** Your stock changes every month. Does your insurance know?
If declared stock is lower than actual stock, the claim can be reduced.
Declare stock accurately and on time, as your policy requires.
**Ask:** Do you know how your policy treats stock?

**73. Documents after a fire**
**Hook:** After a fire, your claim depends on papers that may have burned.
Bills, stock registers and purchase records prove your loss.
Keep digital copies offsite or in the cloud. Update them monthly.
**Ask:** Where are your business records backed up?

**74. Inform the insurer quickly**
**Hook:** The first 24 hours after a fire decide your claim.
Delay in informing the insurer or police can complicate it.
Inform the insurer and police immediately, take photos, don't throw away damaged goods until the surveyor sees them.
**Ask:** Do you have your insurer's claim number saved?

**75. What a surveyor checks**
**Hook:** Here's what a fire surveyor looks at first.
The cause, the extent of loss, the records, and whether the premises matched what was declared.
Keep premises as declared, keep records, and cooperate fully.
**Ask:** What would you want to ask a surveyor?

**76. Changes not declared**
**Hook:** Changed your shop into a godown? Tell your insurer.
If the occupancy or risk changes and isn't declared, it can affect the claim. Check your policy wording.
Inform the insurer of any change in use, construction or stored goods.
**Ask:** Has your business changed since you bought the policy?

**77. Business interruption**
**Hook:** Fire insurance pays for the building. Who pays for the months you're closed?
Standard fire policies cover property. Lost profit needs separate business interruption cover.
Ask your insurer about loss of profit or business interruption cover.
**Ask:** Could your business survive 3 months closed?

**78. Reinstatement value vs market value**
**Hook:** Your claim might pay what your machine is worth today, not what a new one costs.
Market value takes off depreciation; reinstatement value covers replacing new, if your policy has it.
Check which basis your policy uses before a loss, not after.
**Ask:** Do you know which basis your policy uses?

**79. Fire safety and premium**
**Hook:** Better fire protection can lower your premium.
Insurers price risk. Sprinklers, alarms and good housekeeping make you a better risk.
Ask your insurer which protections they consider in rating.
**Ask:** Has anyone ever told you this?

**80. Arson and fraud**
**Hook:** Every fire claim gets a cause investigation. Here's why.
Insurers must rule out deliberate fires, so cause and origin are always checked.
Honest claims go faster when records are complete and the scene is untouched.
**Ask:** Did you know every cause is investigated?

### I. Myth vs fact

**81.** **Hook:** "Water puts out every fire." No. Water on burning oil or live electrical equipment makes it worse. Use a lid or wet chemical for oil; cut power and use CO2 for electrical. **Ask:** Did you believe this one?

**82.** **Hook:** "MCB means my wiring is safe." No. Loose connections overheat without tripping it. Get terminals checked yearly. **Ask:** Did you think the MCB covered everything?

**83.** **Hook:** "Fire spreads slowly, I'll have time." No. A room can become deadly in a few minutes. Get out first, call second. **Ask:** How long do you think you'd have?

**84.** **Hook:** "Smoke is less dangerous than fire." No. Smoke and toxic gases cause most fire deaths. Stay low, cover your mouth, close doors behind you. **Ask:** Did you know smoke kills more than flames?

**85.** **Hook:** "My building is new, so it's fire-safe." Not necessarily. Safety depends on maintenance and how it's used today. Check exits and equipment. **Ask:** Is your new building really safe?

**86.** **Hook:** "Insurance pays everything." No. Sum insured, deductibles, exclusions and declarations all matter. Read your wording. **Ask:** What does your policy not cover?

**87.** **Hook:** "Phone at a petrol pump causes fires." Mostly myth; static and smoking are the real risks. Switch off the engine, no smoking, touch metal first. **Ask:** Which myth did you grow up with?

**88.** **Hook:** "Use the lift, it's faster." Never in a fire. Lifts can stop between floors and shafts fill with smoke. Use the stairs. **Ask:** Did you know this rule?

**89.** **Hook:** "Close the door? Just run." Close it behind you. A closed door slows fire and smoke for everyone still inside. **Ask:** Do you sleep with your bedroom door closed?

**90.** **Hook:** "An extinguisher lasts forever." No. It needs regular checks, servicing and refilling. Check the gauge today. **Ask:** What does your gauge read?

### J. Real incidents, real lessons ⚠

Research each from official reports or major news before shooting. Never
name or blame victims. Lead with the lesson, not the horror.

**91. Uphaar Cinema, Delhi, 1997** ⚠
Lesson: blocked exits and extra seating trap people. Hook idea: "In 1997, a cinema audience couldn't reach the exits. Here's what changed."

**92. Kumbakonam school, Tamil Nadu, 2004** ⚠
Lesson: thatched roofs and a single staircase in a school. Hook idea: "A school fire changed India's building rules for children."

**93. AMRI Hospital, Kolkata, 2011** ⚠
Lesson: storage in a basement meant for parking, smoke through AC ducts. Hook idea: "A hospital basement full of storage. Here's why that matters."

**94. Kamala Mills, Mumbai, 2017** ⚠
Lesson: rooftop restaurants, flammable decor, limited exits. Hook idea: "Two rooftop restaurants. One night. What every diner should check."

**95. Takshashila coaching centre, Surat, 2019** ⚠
Lesson: coaching class in a structure with one escape route. Hook idea: "Students had one way out. Parents, check this today."

**96. Mundka building, Delhi, 2022** ⚠
Lesson: commercial use without fire approvals, one exit. Hook idea: "A four-storey building. One exit. Here's the lesson."

**97. TRP Game Zone, Rajkot, 2024** ⚠
Lesson: temporary structure, combustible materials, hot work reported. Hook idea: "A game zone in a temporary shed. What made it so dangerous."

**98. Jhansi medical college NICU, 2024** ⚠
